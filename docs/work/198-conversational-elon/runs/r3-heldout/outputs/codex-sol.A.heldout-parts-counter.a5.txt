I’d keep this in Excel. For 240 parts on one laptop, Retool/Supabase or Next.js would add hosting, maintenance and a second copy of data to keep in sync without improving the counter lookup.

I’d add a **“Counter lookup” sheet** to your existing workbook:

- One clearly marked cell where you type the part code.
- Large, easy-to-read results for **shelf location** and **selling price**.
- An exact-match lookup with a clear “Part not found” message.
- Protected formula cells so you can’t accidentally overwrite them.

Your existing parts sheet stays the authoritative list. I’d turn its rows into an Excel Table so the lookup automatically includes new parts, then use `XLOOKUP` to retrieve the shelf and price. Part codes should be stored as text to preserve leading zeros; prices should use your currency format. If your Excel version lacks `XLOOKUP`, `INDEX`/`MATCH` will do the same job.

You’d open the workbook at the counter, type a code, read the result, and replace the code for the next customer. It works offline and requires no database or deployment.

I’d consider a separate app when you need multiple counters, remote access, or stock and sales workflows. For your current setup, Excel is the stack I’d choose.
