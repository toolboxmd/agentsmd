`git fetch` downloads commits, branches, and tags from the remote into your local repo's remote-tracking branches (e.g. `origin/main`), but doesn't touch your working branch or files.

`git pull` is essentially `git fetch` followed by a merge (or rebase, if configured) into your current branch — so it does update your working branch.

Rule of thumb: use `fetch` when you want to see what's changed before deciding what to do, and `pull` when you're ready to bring those changes into your current branch.
