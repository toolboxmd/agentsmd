The initials column alone won't meet the contract, but you can keep the folder and add a few pieces that would be shippable this week.

**Where the initials column falls short**

- **Approver identity:** Anyone can type anyone's initials. Nothing ties the entry to the person who actually approved.
- **Approval time:** A typed date can be edited later. There's no trustworthy record of when approval happened.
- **Exact approved version:** Authors can edit the shared folder, so the approved file can change after approval. A naming convention won't prevent that.
- **No self-approval:** Nothing stops an author from entering their own initials.
- **Seven-year retention:** A working folder isn't a retention store. Files can be moved, renamed or deleted.

If the customer audits this, an editable spreadsheet of initials is unlikely to count as evidence of any of these controls.

**A minimal setup that does meet them**

1. **Keep the working folder** as it is for drafting.
2. **Add a separate "Released" location** where only reviewers can write and authors can only read. Turn on versioning and a retention or legal-hold policy of at least seven years. SharePoint, Google Drive (Vault) and S3 Object Lock all support this.
3. **Record approvals through a sign-in, not typing.** For example, use a Microsoft or Google Form that requires login and logs the approver's account and timestamp automatically. It would capture the document name, version and author.
4. **Freeze the approved version.** On approval, the reviewer exports a PDF to the Released location and records its SHA-256 hash in the approval record. Later edits in the working folder can't touch it, and the hash proves it hasn't changed.
5. **Block self-approval.** Reviewers approve only other people's documents. A simple check that the author doesn't equal the approver account can flag violations. If a reviewer also authors documents, another reviewer must approve those.
