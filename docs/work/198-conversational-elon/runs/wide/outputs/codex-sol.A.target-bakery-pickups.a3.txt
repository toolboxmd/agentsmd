I’d make today’s pickups the first priority, then use next week’s partner meeting to design the rollout around preparation deadlines. The shared list is visible and payments and stock reconcile; the immediate gap appears to be how staff choose which cake to prepare next.

For today, filter the list to cakes due today, sort by pickup time, assign each unfinished cake to a named person, and have one person check progress until every cake is ready. If any pickup is at risk, contact that customer early.

I’d structure the Odoo rollout in four stages:

1. **Define the preparation workflow.** Record pickup date and time, preparation start deadline, responsible person, and status. Agree who checks upcoming deadlines and handles overdue work.
2. **Pilot the counter queue.** Show due and overdue cakes first, with clear preparation and ready-for-pickup states. Test that an order entered later for next month cannot displace a cake needed today.
3. **Connect sales, inventory and manufacturing.** Have the partner demonstrate how a prepaid order creates the required production work, schedules it against pickup time, and updates its status at the counter. Verify payment and stock reconciliation remains correct.
4. **Expand after a successful pilot.** Start with a small set of cake orders and a clearly designated source of truth. Go live fully once staff can reliably take every order from entry through preparation to pickup.

Make “every cake ready before its pickup time” the acceptance criterion. Module synchronization supports that outcome, but it won’t establish preparation priorities on its own.
