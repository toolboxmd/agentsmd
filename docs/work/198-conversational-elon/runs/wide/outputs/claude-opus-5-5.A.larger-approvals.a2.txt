I'd advise against shipping that as the control. Initials in a column wouldn't meet the contract, and an auditor would likely spot it quickly.

**Why initials in the shared folder fall short:**

- **Approver identity:** Anyone with edit access can type anyone's initials. Nothing ties the entry to a real, authenticated person.
- **Approval time:** A typed date can be changed later. The file's "modified" time changes with every edit.
- **No self-approval:** Authors can edit the folder, so nothing stops an author from approving their own document. A convention depends on all 15 people following it every week.
- **Exact approved version:** If the approved file stays editable, later edits overwrite what was approved. A naming convention doesn't stop that.
- **Seven-year retention:** Ordinary folders can be cleaned up, reorganised or purged by version-history limits.

**A version that could realistically be ready this week:**

Keep your current folder for drafting, and add a separate release step:

1. **A locked "Released" location.** Only the three reviewers can write to it. Authors can only read it. Your platform's permissions enforce this, not a convention.
2. **Approve by e-signature or a built-in approval flow.** Examples are Adobe Sign, DocuSign, SharePoint/Power Automate approvals, or Google Drive approvals, depending on what you already have. These record a logged-in identity and a server timestamp for you. Make the reviewer, not the author, the required approver.
3. **Freeze the approved version.** Export the approved document to PDF, or use the signed output, and store that copy in Released. Record a checksum if you can. Later edits happen to the draft and never touch the released copy.
4. **Turn on a retention policy or hold** of 7+ years for the Released location, plus audit logging, if your platform supports it. Most business tiers of Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace do.
5. **Keep a simple release register** listing document, version, approver, time and link. Populate it from the approval tool rather than by hand typing.

If you need something for this week's delivery before all of that is set up, you could combine e-signed PDFs, the reviewer-only folder and a written procedure. Tell the customer the retention policy will be added by a specific date. That's honest and defensible. Initials alone are not.
