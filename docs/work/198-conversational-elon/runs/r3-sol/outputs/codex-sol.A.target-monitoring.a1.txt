I’d start with a **dead-man’s-switch alert for `nightly-sync`**. A central log can help diagnose failures, but it won’t tell you that the job never ran unless you add monitoring.

For this one job, I’d use a hosted service such as Healthchecks.io:

- Keep cron and the Python script on `edna`.
- Have cron run a small wrapper that sends a “started” ping, runs the script, then sends “success” only if it exits successfully—or “failure” if it crashes.
- Set the monitor to expect the job daily at 03:00 in your local timezone, with enough grace time for its normal runtime.
- Send alerts to your phone or email when it fails or misses its deadline. That catches an expired token, a stopped cron, or an offline Mac—even when the script never reaches its email step.

Keep the existing summary email for successful runs. Monitoring should be independent of that email path.

The Synology can run **self-hosted Healthchecks in Docker** if you prefer, and we can collect logs there later. But if your home power or internet goes down, a monitor on the NAS may be unable to alert you. An external monitor covers that gap and needs less maintenance.

My recommendation: **add the external heartbeat monitor first, deliberately test a failed run and a missing run, and verify the alerts reach you.** Keep the sync where it is; Docker and central logging can wait until there’s a concrete need.
